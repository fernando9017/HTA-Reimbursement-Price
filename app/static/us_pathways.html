Overview

Clinical pathways are evidence-based treatment decision tools that recommend standardised regimens based on efficacy, toxicity, and cost. In the US, roughly 64% of oncologists are exposed to third-party clinical pathways, and ~86% adherence is reported among those subject to them. NSCLC is one of the top tumour types targeted by pathway programs.

This module tracks the landscape of US NSCLC pathway programs — both payer-driven (insurers mandating on-pathway regimens) and provider/institution-driven (health systems standardising care). Most detailed pathway content is proprietary, but several public and semi-public sources can be monitored.

Pathway Programs Directory

PUBLIC VA Oncology — NSCLC Clinical Pathways

The VA (Department of Veterans Affairs) National Oncology Program publishes freely available clinical pathway PDFs that recommend standard treatments based on efficacy, toxicity, and cost. These are the most comprehensive publicly available US oncology pathways. Updated at least quarterly.

PUBLIC NCI PDQ — NSCLC Treatment Summary

The National Cancer Institute's Physician Data Query (PDQ) provides comprehensive, peer-reviewed, evidence-based treatment summaries that serve as a clinical reference standard. PDQ content is public domain (US government funded) and freely available.

REGISTRATION REQ NCCN Guidelines — NSCLC

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Clinical Practice Guidelines are the gold standard for US oncology treatment decisions. Free with registration, but copyrighted — cannot be scraped or reproduced. Current version: Version 5.2026.

Commercial Pathway Monitoring Landscape

Several commercial platforms offer proprietary pathway monitoring services. While their data is not publicly accessible, understanding the landscape helps identify key information sources and decision-makers.

NSCLC Biomarker-Driven Therapy Landscape

Modern NSCLC treatment is highly biomarker-driven. Pathway programs typically stratify regimens by molecular targets. This reference maps key biomarkers to FDA-approved therapies commonly placed on pathways.
